## Corrections made during validation

- Vite's Windows sandbox file resolution returned EPERM. Tests and builds succeeded with approved execution outside that sandbox; no source workaround was needed.
- Explicit dependency prebundling prevents a first-entry development reload from discarding the selected seat.
- Visual review identified repeated seat geometry translations during media-state rerenders. Seat offsets now live in instance transforms; base geometry stays immutable.
- The seat map projects shared X/Z seat positions, including aisle gaps and variable row spacing; it does not independently generate a rectangular grid.
- The initial all-seat floor-contact check found that the central VP pair crossed the center aisle cut. The paired VP row now has a continuous platform; the central aisle ends at its front edge. All 426 base footprints pass after that correction.
- The viewport browser assertion now waits for R3F's measured canvas size, rather than treating a newly mounted canvas's temporary 300 × 150 default as its final size.
- Media-error recovery checks that the error text disappears after reload; successful playback removes the status element entirely, so it does not require that obsolete element to remain mounted.
- Pointer movement/down wakes controls in the capture phase, before canvas event handling, so the hidden player can reappear reliably. Environment tests wake controls before clicking after screenshots.
- Software WebGL scenes run serially with a 60-second scenario budget to avoid CPU contention between multiple Chrome instances. Nearby chair silhouettes use two curve segments on desktop and one on mobile; distant chairs omit small cup details and use plain faces. All twelve decorative fixture sets remain visible. Textured halos and baked vertex shading provide inexpensive approximate lighting, with two/zero supplemental sconce point lights on desktop/mobile. No bloom or dynamic shadows are used.

## Playback performance correction

- Profiling identified excessive 3D rendering work rather than an unusually demanding source file: the supplied video remains 1546 × 1080 at 24 fps. The original VP12 scene submitted 138 draw calls and 951,810 triangles per frame at 1440 × 900.
- Static floor/rail batching, merged instanced chair parts and distance-dependent chair detail reduce that scene to 28 draw calls and 74,690 triangles (approximately 80% fewer calls and 92% fewer triangles). Seat records, floor bounds, screen dimensions and camera coordinates are unchanged.
- MeshBasic materials with static vertex colors avoid costly per-pixel lighting. House-light transitions tint these materials and the decorative halos; fixed EXIT signs and the video surface are excluded. Paused/playing and both side-wall screenshots were reviewed after the change.
- Timeline and volume updates no longer rerender the auditorium. The Canvas requests the high-performance GPU, disables multisample antialiasing, caps pixel ratio at 1 and lowers it to a minimum of 0.55 when measured frame rate falls below 25. Quality state is controlled by the Canvas parent so media updates cannot reset it.
- An eight-second sample after warmup in local Chrome, using its default NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3050 Laptop GPU, measured approximately 120 rendered frames/second, 8.5 ms p95 frame interval, 193 video frames and zero reported dropped video frames. This is a short local test, not a sustained-performance guarantee for other devices.
- Forced SwiftShader software rendering remains slower: the optimized scene measured approximately 17.8 frames/second at pixel ratio 0.55. Hardware acceleration materially affects playback; browser automation with forced software rendering does not represent normal GPU playback.

## Limits and pending acceptance

- **Resolved earlier defect — ISSUE-PARAGON-001:** all chairs face world −Z with zero yaw as recorded in DL-012; DL-013 removed the VP tables. This refinement preserves those decisions and all 426 seat coordinates. The earlier alignment screenshot remains historical evidence.
- **Paragon opens a functional prototype.** A browser flow verified L4 entry inside the browser tab, video playback on the 3D screen, exit and retained seat code. VP and A–L are present; K starts at 2 and L at 4. The provisional numbering yields 426 selectable IDs, five more than the stated 421. Numbering direction and exact category boundaries remain unresolved; the approved physical screen is 26.3 × 19.8 m (1.328:1), independently of the 1.43:1 video.
- Rake is now 22.5° between row centerlines, compared with the prior 10.19°. It references the 20°–25° range in IMAX Design Facts sheet 1510 (1991); it is not a measured Paragon rake or a current IMAX compliance claim. Ordinary 1.15 m run gives approximately 0.476 m rise. Front centerline starts at 10 m; F/E and A/VP gaps also affect elevations. Cross-gap stairs can be steeper (default maximum approximately 45.24°) because part of their run is a flat landing; their at-most-0.18 m individual rise is a prototype design choice, not a building-code assertion.
- Developer calibration edits/imports affect only the preview and reset on reload; exported JSON does not modify public defaults. Invalid values and non-increasing custom levels preserve the last valid scene. The entire auditorium is still modular JavaScript configuration, not migrated to JSON.
- The user-supplied `Infinite Worlds.mp4` is Paragon's default media. Its 1546 × 1080 source is center-cropped vertically through UVs to the user-confirmed CoLa 1.90:1 frame, 25 × 13.1578947 m centered within the unchanged 26.3 × 19.8 m physical screen. The retained source height is 813.6842105 pixels; the MP4 is unchanged and no stretching occurs. Center placement/cropping remain simulation assumptions rather than verified theatrical framing.
- The public form and scene identify Paragon as a prototype. Ten other carousel entries display Coming Soon and can be selected for browsing, while their combobox/map/entry controls are disabled, including Major Rangsit, which has no auditorium data. Direct Rangsit experience URLs remain unavailable.
- The synthetic development preview tests mechanics only. Its simplified seats, tiers, room and lighting are not a reconstruction of any venue.
- Browser flow automation uses desktop Chrome with software WebGL; mobile testing is viewport emulation. A separate short local hardware-GPU playback sample is recorded above. Real touch-device behavior, Safari/Firefox and sustained frame rates on other hardware have not been benchmarked.
- Aspect fitting is validated mathematically and visually. Apparent screen size depends on seat position and viewport; a narrow portrait viewport may show only part of the physical screen until the viewer looks around. Footage itself is not stretched or cropped on the mesh.
- Camera defaults are not calibrated against real photographs. Lighting is intentionally approximate and does not dynamically reproduce each video's ambient illumination.
- EXIT side/front-row locations are user-confirmed; door dimensions, light-strip count/heights, foam-sheet dimensions/texture, light intensities and the chosen 3 m side clearance are configurable reconstruction estimates. The user clarified that acoustic material is black and the blue appearance comes from illumination. Neutral grayscale textures and separate blue wash meshes implement that distinction without physical light simulation. Procedural textures are original; the source photos/watermarks are not public assets. Calibration JSON remains v1 and does not store architecture or appearance settings.
- npm 8 emits an engine warning for a transitive camera-controls dependency recommending npm 10.5+. Installation, build and tests succeeded; Node 22 with modern npm is the recommended environment.
